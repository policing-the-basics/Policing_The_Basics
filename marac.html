Line of inquiry 01

Surrey Police's MARAC referral rate is 14.4 points below the national average

Multi-Agency Risk Assessment Conferences (MARAC) exist to protect domestic abuse victims at high risk of serious harm. Nationally, the police are the single largest source of referrals into that process. In Surrey, they refer noticeably fewer cases than police forces elsewhere — and no audit exists to check whether cases that should have been referred were missed.

What we already know Surrey Police doesn't hold

From FOI 004984/25 — Surrey Police's own response, 2025

Before finding the national comparator figure above, a direct request was made to Surrey Police for any internal audit of MARAC referral compliance. The relevant part of the reply:

FOI 004984/25 — response, item 7

"Any internal audits, reviews, or reports prepared since 1 January 2020 that evaluate Surrey Police's compliance with MARAC referral requirements" — no information held.

In other words: Surrey Police's own records show no mechanism exists to check whether officers are correctly identifying and referring domestic abuse cases that meet MARAC criteria. The national comparator figure (49.1% vs 63.6%) is the first indication — found in published statistics, not internal Surrey Police data — that this gap in oversight coincides with a real gap in referral rate.

What this evidence does not establish

Read this before drawing conclusions from the numbers above
Caveat

This data cannot tell us why any individual case — including specific cases known to the site's author — was not referred to MARAC. A lower force-wide referral rate is consistent with several explanations: under-referral by officers, more DA cases being handled through other safeguarding routes before reaching MARAC threshold, or differences in how "referral" is recorded between forces. The published data lets us verify that Surrey's rate is genuinely lower — not why.
