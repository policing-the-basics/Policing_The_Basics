What Surrey Police's own FOI answers show

Freedom of Information · Surrey Police, PCC and IOPC

Freedom of Information responses from Surrey Police, the Surrey Police and Crime Commissioner and the IOPC, read together, raise eight concerns about complaints, evidence retention, arrests and domestic abuse safeguarding in Surrey.

  1. Surrey Police FOI refusals contradicted by its own later answers
    It said Regulation 40 complaint suspensions couldn't be searched (12,114 records, 1,000+ hours), then listed them case by case six weeks later. It said no MARAC audit was held while its own intranet cited one.
  2. No record of whether complainants are domestic abuse victims
    Surrey Police's Professional Standards database has no field for domestic abuse victim status, and missed MARAC referrals aren't recorded unless they lead to disciplinary action.
  3. Body-worn video deleted after 30 days, custody CCTV overwritten after 31
    There is no published procedure for preserving BWV or custody CCTV footage when a complaint is made, and no log of custody CCTV deletions.
  4. 132 complaints suspended under Regulation 40, only 11 reviewed by the IOPC
    Complaints about Surrey Police were frozen under Regulation 40 between 2021 and 2025, some for years, with very little outside review. Inspectors found a third of cases that should have gone to the IOPC were never referred.
  5. Up to 14,000 Surrey arrests a year could be unlawful
    They cite "prompt and effective investigation", a reason the High Court has twice found insufficient on its own in Surrey cases (TL 2017, ALK 2025). Surrey only "expects" officers to add specific reasons and has never audited whether they do. The PCC says it has "no evidence" of a problem, the IOPC holds no criteria or audits for checking, and inspectors flagged weak Code G explanations in 2021 but made no recommendation.
  6. FOI requests delayed, refused or labelled "vexatious"
    Requests about evidence, complaints and arrest powers took up to eight months to answer; one has had no reply in over a year. The Information Commissioner served Surrey with an enforcement notice over its FOI backlog in 2024.
  7. False imprisonment and unlawful arrest payouts rising
    Surrey Police's civil claim payouts rose from £110,281 in 2021/22 to £407,687 in 2023/24; false imprisonment claims settled rose from 3 to 12 a year.
  8. Arrests of women up 68% in three years
    Surrey arrested 2,208 women in 2024/25, up from 1,316 in 2021/22. Arrests of men rose 34%; nationally, arrests of women rose 26%.

Sourcing rule

Every statistic carries a stamp naming its table, sheet, or FOI reference. If we can't tag it to a source, it doesn't go on the site.

Correction policy

An earlier draft of the arrest-rate analysis overstated a figure by mis-filtering a spreadsheet. It was corrected before publication — that process is shown on the MARAC and arrest-rate pages, not erased.

What this site does not claim

Statistical patterns are flagged as patterns requiring explanation — not as proof of discrimination or misconduct — unless a named source states otherwise.
